Every team disagrees. The healthy ones disagree about the work, early and in
the open, and then commit to a decision. The unhealthy ones either never
disagree out loud (and the objection surfaces six weeks later as "I always
thought this was a bad idea") or disagree about people, in public, in
writing, forever.

Disagreeing well is a skill with a few concrete moves: make the disagreement
about outcomes and evidence rather than preferences; offer options with
their costs instead of a flat "no"; pick the right channel; know when to
escalate and how to do it without going around someone; and once a decision
is made, commit to it as if it were yours. Mid-level engineers are expected
to push back, and to do it in a way that makes the decision better rather
than slower.
