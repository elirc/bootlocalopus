Most of a distributed team's work happens in writing: Slack threads,
tickets, PR descriptions, status updates, handover notes. A message that
makes three people ask a follow-up question costs a round trip each, and
across time zones a round trip is a day. The engineers who get unblocked
fastest, get their proposals approved, and get trusted with more are
usually not the best coders on the team. They are the ones whose messages
can be acted on **without a reply**.

The habits are simple and learnable: lead with the point, give the context
the reader lacks, say exactly what you need and by when, and make it easy to
say yes. Each question below is a message you will write this month. Some
have more than one correct answer.
